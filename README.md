<h3>🧬 From Distributed Systems to Clinical Medicine</h3>
<p>
    Complex biological and epidemiological systems can’t be reasoned about in isolation. Pathogens propagate across space and time; drugs distribute, bind, and clear through coupled physiological compartments; clinical decisions unfold over noisy, longitudinal patient timelines. Capturing those dynamics requires both production-grade computational rigor and deep biochemical and clinical literacy.
</p>
<p>
    While continuing to work as a software engineer, I am currently completing a <strong>B.S. in Biochemistry (Clinical Pharmacology concentration)</strong> on the pre-medical track—building the wet-lab, physiological, and quantitative foundation for medical school and residency.
</p>

<h3>❄️ Nix &amp; Reproducible Scientific Computing</h3>
<p>
    Nix is the only package manager I’ve used that treats reproducibility as an architectural invariant rather than an afterthought. Immutable derivations, referential transparency, and deterministic builds aren’t academic niceties—they are essential in computational biology, pharmacometrics, and clinical AI, where complex toolchains make "works on my machine" a scientific liability. A result you can’t reproduce isn’t a result.
</p>

    My daily environment runs macOS as the host for graphical workflows alongside a NixOS VM handling development, numerical modeling, and compilation. You can explore my declarative system configuration <a href="https://github.com/johnathan-radojevich/nome">here</a>.
</p>
